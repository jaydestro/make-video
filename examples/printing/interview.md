# Sample interview: The shapes of letters

This is a fictional example of asking a coding agent, “Use make-video. Interview me for a new video.” It isn't a customer transcript.
The answers below are the creative brief for the [finished sample video](sample/video.mp4?raw=true).

**Agent:** What should the video be based on?

**Creator:** I have a [short recording](narration.wav) of Linda Johnson reading a passage about printing from *Arts and Crafts Essays*. Use her recording and keep the words exactly as spoken.

**Agent:** Who's watching, and what's the one thing they should take away?

**Creator:** Visitors at a small library exhibit. I want them to notice how much early printed letters looked like handwriting. Show the difference rather than adding a history lesson.

**Agent:** What language should visitors hear and read?

**Creator:** English for both. People should be able to follow it with the sound off.

**Agent:** Is there an action or a link you want on the last screen?

**Creator:** No call to action. End with the book and reader credited.

**Agent:** Any visual details you'd like me to use or avoid?

**Creator:** Show the same letters twice: *Aa, Bb, Cc*. First make them look handwritten, then set them upright like printed type. No photos or music.

**Agent:** Where will you show it?

**Creator:** A wide exhibit screen. Make one 15-second video with the captions always visible. Leave the last screen up long enough to read the credit.

**Agent:** Here's the cut: the slanted letterforms drift across a dark page during the first phrase. When the reader says “the forms of printed letters,” the same letters settle into upright type. After the narration, the screen holds on *Arts and Crafts Essays, 1893 / Read by Linda Johnson*.
